# STEP RIGHT UP — THREE PITCHES FROM THE RINGMASTER

LADIES AND GENTLEMEN, CHILDREN OF ALL AGES! Gather round, because I have not one, not two, but THREE
spectacular visions for the tutoring website, and every single one of them deserves a drumroll. Do not
adjust your screens. The show is about to begin.

---

## 1. THE GREAT TUTORING CIRCUS

**Vibe:** The entire homepage is one continuous big-top performance, and you, dear visitor, are in the front row.

### What happens as you scroll from top to bottom

The curtains are closed when you arrive, with only a golden slit of light between them and the headline
glowing in the middle like a promise. The moment you scroll, the heavy red drapes part — not all at once,
but slowly, inch by inch, exactly as fast as your finger moves. A spotlight swings across the page and
follows your cursor like an usher with a torch.

Then the acts begin. **ACT I — THE STARS** floats both tutors into the centre ring inside two spotlit
circles. **ACT II — THE PRICE TRAPEZE** is the one I shall be shouting about for years: the prices descend
on real swinging trapezes, GCSE and A-Level on their own bars, £34 and £45 glittering in the spotlight,
with the Physics and Maths placeholders hanging from empty hooks like costumes waiting for their measurements.
**ACT III — THE HUMAN STORY** is the tutors' credentials read out as a ringmaster's introduction, complete
with a graduation photograph and a cycling helmet. **ACT IV — THE 60-MINUTE TIGHTROPE** walks the lesson
structure step by step across a wire, with the plain little label "Approximate timings" pinned beneath it
like a safety notice. **ACT V — THE APPLAUSE** turns the parent reviews into a rising clap-o-meter; the
further you scroll, the louder the audience roars. And in the **FINALE**, the contact section opens its
curtains and the Enquire button takes a bow.

### The moment someone screen-records

The price trapeze. Three rigs swing in from the wings, the ropes creak, the £34 and £45 cards arc back and
forth over the centre ring. It is the only price list on the internet that appears to be performing a
death-defying stunt, and it is impossible not to send to the group chat.

### Why a mum might love it anyway

It is unmistakably warm, it tells her exactly who teaches what and what it costs in enormous friendly
letters, and it makes her child feel like the star of the show rather than a row in a spreadsheet.

## 2. THE HUMAN CANNONBALL PROSPECTUS

**Vibe:** The page is a cannon, the scroll is the fuse, and every section is fired into the sky.

### What happens as you scroll from top to bottom

At the top stands an enormous brass cannon aimed at the corner of the screen. The headline is painted on
its barrel. As you scroll, a fuse lights and burns down the left-hand margin, and a gunpowder gauge fills
with each section you pass. The first scroll fires a smoke ring across the viewport, out of which tumbles
the headline and the two tutor portraits, landing in a heap, dusting themselves off, and forming the hero.

Continue and the price cards are launched one at a time: GCSE Computer Science, A-Level Computer Science,
Physics, Maths, each a cannonball that arcs across the screen and slams into place as a big brass plaque.
The lesson steps are fired as a chain of little cannonballs that bounce into a neat line with the
"Approximate timings" label floating above like a flag. The reviews arrive as a volley of paper confetti,
each quote unfolding mid-air into a readable card. At the bottom the cannon swings to face the viewer, and
the Enquire button sits in the muzzle with a match beside it.

### The moment someone screen-records

Reaching the bottom: the fuse reaches the powder, the screen shakes, and Barney and Eugene are fired clean
across the viewport in a puff of smoke and a shower of paper, landing in the contact section with a "Ready
to get started?" sign. The parent reviews flutter down afterwards like the world's most educational ticker
tape.

### Why a mum might love it anyway

Every fact lands as its own solid, catchable object, the gauge tells her how much of the page is left, and
the whole thing is so gleefully over-the-top that it defuses the awkwardness of shopping for a tutor.

---

## 3. THE MIDNIGHT SIDESHOW

**Vibe:** A moonlit fairground midway where every subject is a curtained booth and every price is a brass plaque.

### What happens as you scroll from top to bottom

You arrive at a dark midway strung with bulbs, the headline written on the back of a wooden ticket booth.
Scrolling walks you down the row of attractions rather than down a page: the booths slide past, one after
another, and the bulbs above each one switch on as it reaches the centre of the screen. The first booth is
**Computer Science with Barney**, the second **Physics & Maths with Eugene**, each with a striped awning
and a velvet curtain that lifts when you hover, revealing the subject, the exam board and the price
engraved on a brass plaque. Poke the curtain and it twitches.

The tutors get their own portrait booths, lit like old carnival photographs, with their credentials on
painted boards. The lesson structure becomes a row of little mechanical dioramas behind glass — recap,
live teaching, practice, wrap up — each one whirring into life as you pass, with "Approximate timings" on
a paper ticket. Further along, a fortune-teller's booth dispenses the parent reviews: pull the velvet rope
and a paper ticket prints out with Rahnuma or Suzanne's full quote on it. The final booth is the ticket
office, where "Ready to get started?" hangs above a proper Enquire button.

### The moment someone screen-records

Pulling the fortune-teller's rope and watching a review physically print out of the booth, then peeling it
off and reading it while the bulbs flicker. Nobody expects a website to have a working ticket machine.

### Why a mum might love it anyway

It is a fairground she can browse at her own pace, each child's subject has its own little shopfront, and
the prices are genuinely easy to find on their brass plaques — the whimsy never gets in the way of the
information.
