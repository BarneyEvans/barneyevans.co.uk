# Wacky harvest

## Ten ideas worth stealing, ranked

1. **A light that travels and bends around content.** Mad Physicist, gravitational lens ([pitch](mad-physicist/pitches.md), [sketch](mad-physicist/sketch/index.html)). A single ray reacting to each section could make the whole page feel physically connected, and the lens moment is screen-recordable. Keep the light soft and behind stable, readable content; no inverted scrolling. **Whole concept.**
2. **Two portraits connected by responsive field lines.** Mad Physicist, magnetic field ([pitch](mad-physicist/pitches.md)). Lines gently reconfigure near a finger, making the partnership feel alive and hinting at physics. Use a few pale curves, equal portraits and a restrained touch response; keep labels and prices fixed. **A moment.**
3. **A page that grows as you scroll.** Maximalist's construction idea ([pitch](maximalist/pitches.md), [sketch](maximalist/sketch/index.html)). Watching a structure assemble gives the visitor a satisfying sense of making something. Translate the bricklayers into a delicate line or shape joining up across sections; never imply Eugene himself is unfinished or obscure placeholders. **Whole concept.**
4. **A gentle scene change midway down the page.** Maximalist's corridor bend ([pitch](maximalist/pitches.md)). A surprising turn creates a memorable journey and makes the second half feel genuinely different. Let a background path curve around a corner while the document scrolls normally; keep text upright and avoid a literal school corridor. **Whole concept.**
5. **A shared path through the lesson steps.** Ringmaster's tightrope ([pitch](ringmaster/pitches.md), [sketch](ringmaster/sketch/index.html)). A thread travelling from recap to wrap-up turns a list into an easy story. Draw a fine curve behind four stationary steps, explicitly Barney's, with approximate timings in text and no proportional bar. **A moment.**
6. **A small spotlight that follows a finger.** Ringmaster's usher ([pitch](ringmaster/pitches.md), [sketch](ringmaster/sketch/index.html)). Touching the page leaves an immediate, playful response that feels personal. Make it a faint warm pool on an empty background, never over text or a control, and offer the same still composition without motion. **A moment.**
7. **Two paired elements affect one another.** Mad Physicist's entanglement ([pitch](mad-physicist/pitches.md)). Touch one decorative shape and its companion answers: a tiny surprise worth showing someone. Pair equal-coloured shapes near the tutors, not their prices; never generate or resolve unknown figures. **A moment.**
8. **A soft page surface changes as you travel.** Maximalist's starfield-to-notebook transition ([pitch](maximalist/pitches.md), [sketch](maximalist/sketch/index.html)). The feeling of entering a new chapter gives the scroll a beginning and middle. Blend two quiet warm textures or colours behind static copy, with enough contrast and no retro clutter. **A moment.**
9. **A portrait seems to lift from the page.** Toddler's pop-up cinema ([pitch](toddler/pitches.md)). A slight dimensional shift makes the real people feel present and invites a second look. Give both photos the same subtle scroll-linked depth, without cropping faces or delaying their appearance; flatten for reduced motion. **A moment.**
10. **A review arrives like a tangible keepsake.** Ringmaster's printed ticket ([pitch](ringmaster/pitches.md)). A little paper edge extending beside an already-readable quote makes the testimonial feel human and shareable. Keep the full quote, attribution and MyTutor link visible without pulling anything; use one quiet animation and no carnival booth. **A moment.**

All motion should preserve native scrolling, work on touch, stop off-screen and in hidden tabs, and have a complete still reduced-motion version; provide a pause for any autonomous motion lasting over five seconds.

## Three for Cellular Garden

- Let one soft light travel down the garden and gently change the colour of nearby cells at three distinct scroll positions; text stays still and readable.
- Connect two equal portrait-adjacent cells with a few curved strands; touch one cell and the other gives a restrained answering pulse.
- Grow a single stem through Barney's four lesson steps, adding a leaf at each; timings stay as text and the stem is not a duration chart.

## Ignore

- **Ringmaster:** the dark circus, swinging prices, applause meter and sound make buying tutoring feel like a noisy show.
- **Mad Physicist:** black holes, jargon, moving text, fabricated price possibilities and inverted scroll sacrifice trust and usability for a physics joke.
- **Maximalist:** fake counters, blinking retro clutter, construction workers on Eugene and pretend chat statuses make the page look unreliable.
- **Toddler:** wobbling faces, unreadable animated text, potato mascots and forced wheel navigation will make students cringe and parents work harder.

## Invented or unsupported claims to avoid

- Toddler's [sketch](toddler/sketch/index.html) gives Barney the wrong address `barney@example.com` and invites enquiries about availability. Eugene's example address is a placeholder, not a real address.
- Mad Physicist's [pitch](mad-physicist/pitches.md) proposes fictitious Eugene prices of £28, £35, £42 and £50; its [sketch](mad-physicist/sketch/index.html) adds “active research” and physics-flavoured claims not in the approved copy. Do not turn `£XX/hour` into a number.
- Ringmaster's [sketch](ringmaster/sketch/index.html) says lessons are built around “the student in front of us”, implying the claim applies to both tutors; the supplied lesson descriptions are Barney's. Its promise that “the next performance begins” after an enquiry is also invented.
- Maximalist's [pitch](maximalist/pitches.md) suggests a visitor counter, “Eugene is typing” and “Barney (Online)”: none is a verified live status or count. Its [sketch](maximalist/sketch/index.html) implies a guestbook exists or accepts entries; it does not appear in the supplied copy.
