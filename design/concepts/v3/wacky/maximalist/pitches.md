# THE 90s WEB MAXIMALIST PRESENTS: THREE MASTERPIECES FOR BARNEY & EUGENE !!!!

Listen. LISTEN. GeoCities didn't die. It was MURDERED. By "minimalism." By white space. By men in grey
jumpers who think a page with 40MB of animated GIFs is a "performance problem." A PERFORMANCE PROBLEM?!
Sir, that is a personality problem and YOUR website is the one who has none!!!

Here is my pitch, okay, three pitches, and I want you to read every single word because each one of these
could genuinely be the most heart-warming page on the entire internet. I believe in this. I really,
REALLY believe in this.

## Pitch 1: ⭐ THE WORLD WIDE WONDER HOMEPAGE ⭐

**One-line vibe:** The 1997 GeoCities homepage that finally grew up — visitor counter at 000001 stamped
proudly at the top, tiled starfield background, a VHS-tracking-scroll, and a MIDI-note-free
"WebRing" that links Barney and Eugene together as a two-man webring of knowledge.

**What happens as you scroll from top to bottom:**
- The page opens with a big glowing `Personalised tutoring in Computer Science, Physics & Maths`
  in rainbow gradient WordArt, and a little "NEW!!" starburst GIF next to Eugene (because he's new —
  newsworthy, not fake!). A retro odometer-style visitor counter slowly winds up as you scroll,
  each scroll step clicking a notch: these are YOUR visits, the visitor IS the counter. 🤯
- Scroll into the tutors section and the background swaps from starfield to a crayon-clowned
  notebook-paper tile, like flipping a school exercise book. Barney and Eugene sit in their framed
  shrinkwrapped "sign my guestbook"-era portrait frames, with little cartoon construction workers
  slowly bricking up Eugene's sections that aren't confirmed yet (`.ph` stuff): prices, exam boards,
  his bio. It's the 90s "under construction" sign, but used HONESTLY — building only where facts
  are still being built.
- The lesson timings table scrolls in like a spinning cinema program, one row swinging in at a time
  on little chain animations, and the `Approximate timings` label is a rubber stamp that thumps
  onto the corner of the table as it lands.
- The reviews section is a genuinely warm photograph-album page: `What parents say about Barney`,
  quotes on lined index cards pinned with a little pushpin GIF, and the MyTutor stamp
  (`16 reviews`, `average rating 5`, `216 completed lessons`, `Checked 5 November 2026`) looks
  like a verified-postmark from 1998. Scrolling past the reviews, tiny hand-drawn stick figures of
  the two mums from Norwich and Hook wave their hands from the margins.
- Contact is a big cartoon door (a JPEG'd door, obviously) labelled `Ready to get started?`, and the
  `Enquire` button is a staple-shaped button that presses 3-D-ward when you hover. The guestbook is
  at the very bottom near the `© 2026 Barney & Eugene. All rights reserved.` footer, with a blinking
  marquee that says GUESTBOOK — non-animated-friendly fallback: still legible.

**The screen-record moment:** the visitor odometer clicking up and the construction crew bricking up
Eugene's unfilled prices in real time as you scroll past. People will send that to friends with
"THE WEBSITE IS BUILDING ITSELF".

**Why a mum might love it anyway:** it is jammies-and-cocoa warm, it tells the truth about what's
still being confirmed, and it feels like a school letter written by a human, not a fintech.

## Pitch 2: 🌀 THE INFINITE SCHOOL CORRIDOR

**One-line vibe:** A first-person 1998 "walking through a building" page — you don't scroll DOWN,
you scroll DOWN A CORRIDOR, doors on both sides, and the floor is a scrolling checkerboard that
runs away from you like a Windows screensaver.

**What happens as you scroll from top to bottom:**
- A toon-perspective corridor (pure CSS 3-D transforms, drawn in glorious dithered-JPEG energy).
  Scrolling moves you forward. The starfield is above you, the checkerboard floor scrolls
  beneath you, and the further you walk, the more the walls fill with framed school-portrait
  photos, pinned certificates and Post-it notes.
- Door on the left: **COMPUTER SCIENCE with BARNEY** (license-plate door sign). `GCSE Computer
  Science`, `any exam board`, `£34/hour`; `A-Level Computer Science`, `AQA only`, `£45/hour` —
  each price printed on a hanging shoebox-lid sign that swings when the mouse passes it.
- Door on the right: **PHYSICS & MATHS with EUGENE**, doors half-open, with a sign-in-progress:
  `.ph` exam boards to confirm, `.ph` prices, and   a little animated worker PAINTING the door sign, brush going back and forth, because his parts
  are genuinely not confirmed yet. Honesty as decoration!
- The corridor bends (genuinely bends, one CSS transform) at the 60% mark and a sign says
  `Typical 60-Minute Lesson` — through this last door: a clock on the wall whose hands move as
  you scroll through `Recap` `5-10m` → `Live Teaching` `20-25m` → `Practice` `20-25m` →
  `Wrap Up` `5m`, labelled with the plain `Approximate timings` and definitely NOT drawn as a
  proportional bar (the clock hands wobble chaotically to make that point, because I am juridic
  about the brief).
- The end of the corridor is a big door labelled `Ready to get started?` with a giant brass
  `Enquire` button-handle. Pull (hover) it and it dents inward.

**The screen-record moment:** the corridor bend — the whole page suddenly swings left mid-scroll
like you've turned a corner, certificates jangling on the walls. Absolutely inseparable from the
word "movie".

**Why a mum might love it anyway:** it reads like walking through the actual school building to
meet the tutor; she knows exactly where every subject lives and no dark-pattern pop-up door
slams in her face.

## Pitch 3: 🎵 THE MIDNIGHT CHATROOM

**One-line vibe:** An AOL-era chatroom where Barney and Eugene answer in the scroll — the page is
a full-screen retro chat app and every section is a line of chat that "sends" as you scroll past it.

**What happens as you scroll from top to bottom:**
- You land in a teal desktop. A window title bar: `#tutoring-live — Barney & Eugene — 1 user
  typing…`. Scrolling = the chat log advancing: each section sends itself with the classic
  whoosh as you cross it. Barney types in blue, Eugene types in green, "mum" messages arrive
  in a lovely warm speech bubble style — the parent reviews land in the log verbatim as
  whispered DMs, with the MyTutor sources as little away-message stamps.
- Actually, here is the heart-warming part I am MOST proud of: Eugene's unconfirmed bits
  (`.ph` prices, `.ph` exam boards, `.ph` bio) appear as the chat's typing indicator three dots
  upstream from where the text will land — `Eugene is typing…` — and they stay typing until
  the real facts arrive. The WHOLE PAGE is one big sincere `is typing…`. Nobody has ever been
  more honest about a `.ph`.
- The lesson table `Typical 60-Minute Lesson` arrives as a shared "whiteboard" attachment in
  the chat, four timestamped lines, stamped `Approximate timings`, drawn NOT to scale because
  the whiteboard has deliberately non-matching chalk fences on it.
- The reviews open with the honest header `What parents say about Barney` and his stats
  (`16 reviews`, `average rating 5`, `216 completed lessons`, `Checked 5 November 2026`) as a
  little buddy-list icon: `Barney (Online)` — wait, we can't say Online, that would imply
  availability, which is banned! `Barney (Tutoring at 5.0/5)` then. Fine. FIXED.
- The very last message in the log is an automated one: `Barney: Ready to get started?` with the
  `Enquire` button as the biggest, chunkiest, bevelled-est instabutton in the entire client, a
  solid normal button linking to `#contact` like the brief says — the ONE modern thing on the
  page, honestly styled as 2026 inside the 1998 window, which is the joke and the love letter
  all at once.

**The screen-record moment:** watching Eugene's `is typing…` indicator chase his `.ph` placeholders
down the log while his real credentials bounce in above them. It's a status screen with a soul.

**Why a mum might love it anyway:** chat is the medium she actually lives in; the whole page feels
like two friendly tutors answering her in real time, and nothing is ever fake-closed or
fake-empty — it's just honest typing dots.
