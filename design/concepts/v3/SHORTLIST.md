# V3 shortlist — a living garden, with the people in front

**Build [G2 Light Garden](garden/G2-light-garden/index.html): it best combines a reassuring welcome, immediately useful prices and a distinctive world worth remembering.**

**Borrow G1's human opening and lesson warmth; make G2's travelling light connect the whole journey more clearly.**

**First remove the mobile control obstruction, expose essential credentials without a flip, and complete Eugene's offer before treating this as a launch candidate.**

## What this judgment rests on

I read the brief, approved copy, [parent panel](reviews/parent-panel.md), [checker](reviews/checker.md), concept pitches and [harvest](wacky/HARVEST.md). I opened all nine local concepts in headless Chromium at 390 × 844 and 1440 × 844, traversed each page to its end, and inspected five phone and three desktop scroll captures per concept. Phone runs used 4× CPU throttling. I exercised garden profile reveals, warm drawing buttons and wildcard controls, and sent pointer/touch input to the physics scenes. All 18 main page runs recorded no JavaScript errors. G2's opened Eugene profile was legible after its transition at 390px; the fixed controls still covered part of the following profile.

I also inspected the Mad Physicist, Maximalist and Ringmaster sketches at three positions each: these contain the harvest's highest-ranked sources. Screenshots stayed outside the repository. This is a sampled creative review, not real parent research, exhaustive accessibility testing or a physical-phone performance certification. The panel is explicitly simulated; its scores do not measure conversion. The checker supplies the broader 320px, reduced-motion and content checks.

The ranking weighs the concept worth developing, rather than only which current prototype has the fewest obstacles. A correct, calm site still needs an identity; a memorable site still has to let a parent read.

## All nine, ranked

### 1. [G2 — Light Garden](garden/G2-light-garden/index.html)

*A luminous, grown-up garden: the closest synthesis of Barney's two previous favourites.*

**Signature:** a soft optical field accompanies the living cells through the page; a tutor portrait dissolves into its species before revealing the person behind it, and the page ends in lilac dusk.

**Protect:** the early price summary, open subject rows, cream-to-lilac progression, equal tutor treatment and genuine computational behaviour. It feels cared for without looking like a children's learning app. The comparatively compact phone page helps the complete story land.

**Biggest weakness:** the garden UI puts the experiment in front of the reader. The fixed toolbar covers portrait labels and later content; desktop also gives the abstract lens the first introduction before the real people.

**Three changes:**

1. Move planting/species controls into an optional in-flow garden area. Keep Pause readily reachable in a small reserved header space, with no overlay on copy or actions.
2. Bring two equal, named portraits into the desktop opening and preserve their early phone appearance. Put each tutor's main qualification and DBS status in ordinary visible text; reserve the flip for extra detail and keep its animation clear of text.
3. Give the travelling light three unmistakable jobs: meet the paired portraits, follow Barney's four lesson markers, then settle beside contact. Reduce competing contour lines and background glows so that this one journey is perceptible.

### 2. [G1 — Greenhouse](garden/G1-greenhouse/index.html)

*A welcoming glasshouse, with the strongest sense of a place inhabited by real people.*

**Signature:** the seeded garden develops through the page as morning light becomes a golden contact arch; the lesson stem turns the practical sequence into part of that environment.

**Protect:** the paired arched portraits, restrained serif, sage/lavender balance and the prominently attributed “We move at the speed the student needs to fully understand.” That is the emotional centre the brief needs.

**Biggest weakness:** its generous opening puts the phone price summary after the portraits, while the persistent planting bar makes the garden feel like an app layered over the website.

**Three changes:**

1. Place the concise subject/rate summary immediately after Enquire and before the portrait arch on mobile, retaining both names and faces within the first two screens.
2. Apply G2's proposed in-flow planting controls and unobstructive Pause treatment; remove the toolbar's competition with prices and lesson text.
3. Reduce the large subject panels to open rows and tighten the gaps between chapters. Let the changing light and lesson stem supply structure instead of repeated rectangular surfaces.

### 3. [B2 — Wave Study](physics/B2-wave-study/index.html)

*Soft, contemporary science with enough maturity for sixth form.*

**Signature:** paired waves turn into sampled marks, then regain coherence at contact. This can express both tutors' disciplines through one visual idea.

**Protect:** rose/sky/plum, the two equal portrait medallions, readable open price bands and substantial human biography sections. It is warmer than Aperture without resorting to nursery-like drawing.

**Biggest weakness:** the strongest wave is in the opening; later thin scores can feel like section separators. The centred introduction spends space before the price facts, and the lower page is long.

**Three changes:**

1. Move the compact rate summary next to or directly below the opening action on mobile; reduce the gap above the portraits rather than shrinking their faces.
2. Carry one recognisable wave into the sampling transition and lesson markers, increasing its visible change at those points while keeping text stationary. Give the contact resolution a clear relation to that same wave.
3. Tighten biography and inter-section spacing, keeping core credentials visible and longer experience in native disclosures. Darken decorative section numbers if they are intended to be read, as the checker notes.

### 4. [C1 — Soft Assembly](wildcard/C1-soft-assembly/index.html)

*Friendly geometric pieces that acquire a purpose as you travel.*

**Signature:** tiles become a bridge, four stair-like groups, quotation marks and finally an open doorway. Of the non-garden concepts, this offers the clearest series of different visual events.

**Protect:** the periwinkle/apricot identity, sturdy readable type, stable equal portraits and the change in meaning of the same pieces. The ending feels welcoming without making an unsupported promise.

**Biggest weakness:** the visual events occupy separate bands between useful content. On a phone, the page asks for too much scrolling before the next fact; the price summary is below the opening portraits.

**Three changes:**

1. Put the summary immediately below Enquire, with separate GCSE and A-Level lines that do not wrap into a sentence.
2. Reuse the same small cluster across the chapter boundaries visually: position the bridge at the profile entrance, stairs beside the actual lesson steps and quotation shapes beside the full reviews. Remove the empty exhibition space around each scene.
3. Reduce the large rounded lesson/contact containers and their internal padding. Keep the colour changes, but make the composition feel like one page rather than successive oversized cards.

### 5. [A1 — Good Thread](warm/A1-good-thread/index.html)

*A warm, stitched welcome with an immediately human voice.*

**Signature:** the connecting thread becomes a seam through Barney's lesson steps and resolves into a knot at contact; plucking it adds a small tactile response.

**Protect:** raspberry actions, legible friendly type, equal faces and the explicit student-paced lesson quote. The shared path is useful rather than merely ornamental.

**Biggest weakness:** the thick pastel portrait surrounds and very rounded type together lean young; prices remain below the portrait composition on mobile.

**Three changes:**

1. Move and enlarge the rate summary above the portraits, splitting the two Barney rates into separate lines.
2. Remove the yellow inner portrait borders and excess surrounding blobs; retain one fine stitched contour and equal photo sizes.
3. Make the thread physically meet the lesson markers and one review edge more decisively, then simplify its final knot. Strengthen the story through those changes rather than adding more doodles.

### 6. [B1 — Aperture](physics/B1-aperture/index.html)

*Precise, credible and efficient, with the emotional temperature of a small technical consultancy.*

**Signature:** the optical beam branches into the subjects and continues through the lesson sequence before gathering at contact.

**Protect:** the excellent opening hierarchy: subjects, prices, Enquire and both people are quickly available. Keep its stable labels and clear allocation of subjects.

**Biggest weakness:** clarity exceeds character. Repeated fine lens diagrams are tasteful but too similar to give the lower page a memorable development.

**Three changes:**

1. Borrow G1's approved, attributed student-paced quote for the lesson opening and warm the large pale surfaces with a restrained cream/apricot balance.
2. Replace repeated lens-divider scenes with one continuous beam that visibly changes at subjects, lessons and contact; keep the existing useful branches.
3. On narrow screens, stack the subject heading above its GCSE/A-Level rates rather than making the subject title and rate details compete in narrow side-by-side columns.

### 7. [A2 — Little by Little](warm/A2-little-by-little/index.html)

*A calm blue drawing that reassures more than it surprises.*

**Signature:** the loose connector between the tutors settles into an ordered path through the lesson and a relaxed closing underline.

**Protect:** easy reading, the absence of technical intimidation, the attributed teaching approach and the clean blue/apricot palette.

**Biggest weakness:** its progression is too subtle to carry the site's identity. Once the opening scribble passes, the blue panels and recurring lines feel familiar; the squeezed rate summary does not compensate.

**Three changes:**

1. Bring the mobile summary above the portraits and give each level/rate its own readable row.
2. Replace the small two-column phone pricing detail with full-width level rows, retaining the clear subject grouping.
3. Develop the loose-to-ordered idea into three visible changes tied to content: a paired response near the faces, a drawn path meeting the lesson steps and a final resolved shape at contact. Cut decorative lines that do not participate.

### 8. [C2 — Counterpart](wildcard/C2-counterpart/index.html)

*Two soft ribbons in a spacious, elegant but repetitive duet.*

**Signature:** separate paths cross, braid and settle into parallel strokes at the two contact routes.

**Protect:** the equal partnership, rose/plum restraint and the quiet parallel ending. The over/under weave is a useful interaction ingredient.

**Biggest weakness:** the loops repeat their visual argument, while fine type and long decorative bands reduce phone legibility and pace.

**Three changes:**

1. Increase body/label weight and size where the phone view feels faint; distinguish price figures more strongly from supporting labels.
2. Move the opening price summary above the portraits and remove the space allocated to the ribbon-swap prompt in the main reading route.
3. Keep only three decisive ribbon states—separate, braided, parallel—and attach them to portraits, the shared page journey and contact. Remove the other full-width loop interludes.

### 9. [G3 — Lab Notebook](garden/G3-lab-notebook/index.html)

*An intriguing science exhibit whose notebook apparatus overwhelms the welcome.*

**Signature:** the same cellular world magnifies down to nuclei, then pulls back to the whole dish; tutor slides turn through their species' cells.

**Protect:** the coherent change of scale and the genuine shared simulation. These are stronger ideas than the visual notebook packaging around them.

**Biggest weakness:** graph paper, tilted specimen slides, handwriting, chapter notation, magnification and persistent controls all ask for attention. It also runs into the brief's objection to print metaphors.

**Three changes:**

1. Remove graph paper, handwritten asides and most specimen notation; put equal upright portraits and plain subject labels first.
2. Move planting and magnification controls out of the reading overlay; retain the scale change as an occasional background event around empty space.
3. Fix the checker's measured 4.40:1 `.pencil` label contrast if any such labels survive, and enlarge the small supporting text. Preserve the microscope idea as a borrowed moment rather than pursuing the whole notebook direction.

## The four worth developing, and what to combine

**G2 + G1 is my recommended single route.** Use G2's compact structure, early prices, open subject layout and lilac ending; use G1's equal portrait welcome and humane lesson emphasis. Take the harvest's travelling light as the organising motion: it touches a pair of cells near the tutors, grows a stem beside Barney's four stationary steps, then warms the contact area. G2 already attempts the travelling caustic, so refine its readability rather than adding a second effect. A touch on one decorative tutor-adjacent cell can gently answer in the other, borrowing the harvest's paired response without moving faces or prices.

**Keep G1 as the warmer alternative within that route.** If Barney prefers a recognisable place over G2's optical abstraction, retain the glasshouse and golden ending. Import G2's information hierarchy and one restrained lens moment at the meeting of the two colonies. Do not run two independent garden visual systems or add extra controls. This should be a choice of atmosphere, not another complete build.

**B2 is the strongest distinct science alternative.** Borrow B1's opening information hierarchy and content-connected branches, then use the harvest's gentle scene change to turn the broad hero wave into discrete samples by the lesson section. Keep the pink-to-blue journey and both tutors' human presence. Do not add garden cells, ribbon swapping or a third scientific metaphor.

**C1 is the strongest distinct playful alternative.** Take the Maximalist harvest's assembly idea seriously: pieces should appear to make the next useful part of the page. Borrow A1's lesson-path placement so the four little constructions sit beside the actual steps, and Ringmaster's keepsake idea only as a quiet edge accompanying an already-readable, fully attributed Barney review. The doorway can remain the ending. Avoid a construction-site joke or anything suggesting Eugene is unfinished.

I agree with the panel's G2 winner and G3 last place. I move **G1 from sixth to second** because its warmth and recognisable environment are difficult to invent later, while price placement and toolbar obstruction have concrete fixes. I move **C1 from seventh to fourth** because its changing shapes offer more memorable development than another polite line treatment. I move **A2 from second to seventh** and **B1 from third to sixth**: their usability matters, but the brief also demands something a parent can describe to a friend. These are creative-development judgments, not claims that the current versions would outperform the panel's choices in real testing.

## Stop doing this

- Stop equating a repeated line, curve or lens between sections with a continuous story. It needs to change what it does in relation to the content, not just its geometry.
- Stop placing play controls over the information. Touch delight should live in spare space; Pause should remain reachable without hiding a name, price or email.
- Stop spending the first phone screen on a large headline and decorative breathing room while rates sit below the portraits. Two people, subjects, rates and an action must share the opening hierarchy.
- Stop treating tiny text as refinement. Several pages are technically uncluttered but physically demanding on a phone.
- Stop repeating large introductions, portraits, quotations and generous interludes until a simple offer becomes a long scroll. Keep full required reviews and claims; trim repetition and spacing first.
- Stop importing the wacky sketches' surface style. The black hole, fake visitor counter and circus framing undermine the trust the harvested movement ideas could support. None of their invented claims should travel with the visual ideas.

This round has corrected the cold palettes and static pages. It still often places animation beside the content instead of making a small number of meaningful moments organise the journey. Equal-sized photos also cannot make the offer equally complete while one tutor's prices, biography and real contact route are missing.

## Decisions and material Barney needs next

This is the consolidated list; the same placeholder repeated across nine concepts needs supplying only once.

1. **Direction and atmosphere:** approve the recommended G2/G1 combination, choose the warmer G1 variant, or select the distinct B2 or C1 route. Do one focused refinement, not nine further builds.
2. **Final site name:** confirm or replace the working “Barney & Eugene” name for branding and the copyright line.
3. **Eugene's four offers:** supply GCSE Physics, A-Level Physics, GCSE Maths and A-Level Maths rates, with the supported exam boards for each. Confirm whether any identical rates can legitimately share a summary.
4. **Eugene's lesson format:** confirm or replace the assumed “1-to-1, online via Zoom.” Supply his own teaching-method wording if a shared lesson section is wanted; the existing first-person copy and coding timetable remain Barney's until then.
5. **Eugene's biography:** provide the promised short account of how he teaches Physics and Maths. His existing qualifications and Ritz experience can remain the factual foundation; no review quota is needed to manufacture symmetry.
6. **Real enquiry route:** supply Eugene's genuine email and decide whether launch uses two direct emails or a working enquiry form. If a form is wanted, define its recipient/routing and necessary fields; the current `#contact` placeholder is not a form submission service.
7. **Portrait preference, optional:** keep the approved real photos for refinement. If the graduation/helmet mismatch bothers the tutors, supply a matched pair of natural portraits with comparable framing and light; replacing Eugene alone would risk reducing the equal treatment.
8. **Final validation:** put the single revised direction in front of actual parents and students, asking them to identify each tutor's subject, level, rate and contact route without prompting, then ask what they remember. Test the chosen motion on a real mid-range phone, at 320px, with keyboard and reduced motion; carry forward the checker's specific contrast fixes and recheck the moved controls and expanded profiles. Those are implementation checks, not reasons to commission another concept round.
